She was 80. It was her first tattoo. She laughed through the whole thing.

By Swarain Pawar  |  Tiger Skin Tattoos, Naupada, Thane  |  July 2026

Vedashree has been coming to me for years. Six tattoos in, she knows exactly what she wants, sits perfectly, and leaves happy every time. Her mother came to me too. So when she messaged saying she wanted to bring someone special in, I assumed it was a friend.

It was her Mai. Her grandmother. 80 years old. First tattoo.

She walked in with Vedashree and Ajoba, her husband of many decades, who is 86. Two people who have seen more of life than most of us will ever see, walking into a tattoo studio in Naupada, Thane like it was the most natural thing in the world.

I asked Mai what she wanted. She told me her idea simply and clearly. No second-guessing. No "I'm not sure." She had been thinking about what she believed in, what meant enough to carry forever. I made the design. She looked at it, nodded, and sat down.

That's when we got nervous. Not her. Us.

She's 80, I kept thinking. What if it's too much? What if the pain is too intense? What if her skin reacts? What if she needs a break halfway through? I was more anxious than she was. My hands were steady but my mind wasn't.

She didn't need a break.

She sat through the entire session with Vedashree on one side and Ajoba on the other. And she was laughing. Not nervous laughter. Genuine, happy, completely at ease laughter. She was having the time of her life. Ajoba sat with this quiet pride on his face, watching his wife of a lifetime do something she had decided to do for herself. Vedashree was trying not to cry.

I was trying not to cry too, if I'm being honest.

When it was done, both of them looked at me and Ajoba held his wife's hand and they both said it together.

"Tumhch sagl changlch honar ahe. Just keep doing the good work." — Mai and Ajoba, Tiger Skin Tattoos, Naupada, Thane

Everything good will happen for you. Just keep doing the good work.

I've been tattooing for 8 years. I've done over 2,000 pieces. I've won national convention awards. I've built a studio I'm genuinely proud of in Naupada. But that moment, the two of them standing there blessing us, is one of the most meaningful things that has happened to me in this work.

Mai wasn't getting a tattoo because it was trendy. Not because her friends were doing it. She was 80 years old and she decided, at this point in her life, to do something entirely for herself. Something that reflected what she believed in. Something permanent. And she did it without fear, without hesitation, without a single complaint.

That's the thing about tattooing that people on the outside don't always see. It's not about being edgy or rebellious or young. It's about deciding what matters enough to carry forever. Mai knew what that was. She just needed someone to draw it.

We shared this moment on Instagram. The reel got close to a million views. People from all over commented with their own stories, their own grandparents, their own moments they wished they had captured. Something in Mai's decision touched something universal. You can find us at @tigerskintattoos.

But for me, the memory I carry isn't the view count.

It's the studio, quiet, just the four of us, and those six words.

Changlch honar ahe. It will be good.
