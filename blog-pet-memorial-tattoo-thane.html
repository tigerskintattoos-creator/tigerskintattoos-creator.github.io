Pet Memorial Tattoos in Thane: Keeping Them Close

Some of the most meaningful pieces we do are for pets. Here's what to think about before you get one: the design, the photo, the size, and how to make sure it still looks like them years from now.

By Swarain · Tiger Skin Tattoos, Naupada, Thane West · October 14, 2026

People usually walk in with a phone full of photos and a story. A dog who waited at the door every evening for fourteen years. A cat who slept on the same chair through college and two house moves. By the time they sit down, the tattoo is already decided. What they want from us is to get the face right.

That is the whole job with a pet memorial tattoo. A generic dog will not do. It has to be their dog, the ear that never stood up straight, the grey around the muzzle, the look they gave you.

Choose the kind of memorial first

A realistic portrait is the most personal option. Fur direction, eye reflections and expression all come from your photos, so the result looks like your pet and nobody else's. This needs size, which we cover below.

A paw print, nose print or name is quieter and can be small. Many people combine a real paw print with dates or a short line, like the "Always and forever" piece in our portfolio.

An illustrative or line piece captures their shape and personality in a few lines. It suits people who want something softer than a full portrait, and it works well on smaller placements.

The photo matters more than anything

A tattoo can only be as accurate as the reference. The best photos are sharp, in natural light, with the eyes clearly visible and the face filling most of the frame. Avoid heavy filters and photos taken in dark rooms.

If your favourite photo is blurry, send it anyway along with a few clearer ones. We often use one photo for the expression and another for the fur and markings. Part of the consultation is going through them together and picking what will translate best to skin.

Size and placement

Realistic fur and eyes need room. Around 3×3 inches is the smallest size we recommend for a true portrait, and 4 to 5 inches gives the detail space to breathe and age well. Forearm, upper arm, calf and chest are the most common spots because they are flat enough for a face to sit naturally.

Paw prints, names and outlines can go almost anywhere: wrist, ankle, collarbone or behind the ear.

Black and grey or colour?

Black and grey is the most popular choice for memorial portraits. It is timeless, holds detail well on Indian skin tones, and keeps the focus on the expression. Colour suits pets with striking eyes or markings, like a ginger cat or a blue-eyed husky, and works best at a larger size.

What it costs at Tiger Skin

Small memorial pieces such as a paw print, name or outline start from ₹2,000. A detailed pet portrait around palm size usually runs ₹8,000–₹15,000 depending on detail. Larger portraits with backgrounds are booked as full-day sessions at ₹30,000–₹35,000. You get a confirmed quote at the free consultation, before anything is booked.

Take your time

There is no rush with a memorial piece. Some people come in a week after losing their pet, others wait a year. Both are fine. When you are ready, send us your photos on WhatsApp and we will talk it through. See more on our pet portrait tattoo page.
